\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

This chapter introduces the background and motivation for the \gls{SafeVision} project. It presents the need for automated detection and \gls{anonymization} of sensitive information in visual media, outlines the main problem addressed by the project, and defines the project goals, scope, and delimitations. The chapter also describes the expected significance of the work and provides an overview of the thesis structure.

\section{Background}

Sensitive information is increasingly present in digital visual media. Photographs and images are considered widely used across domains such as journalism, surveillance, documentation, mapping, and social platforms. While visual data offers significant practical value, it also introduces challenges related to privacy, security, and ethical responsibility. Images may contain sensitive information, including faces, license plates, textual content, and contextual details that can directly or indirectly identify individuals \cite{frome2009large}.

When such information is disclosed without proper handling, it may lead to privacy violations, unauthorized identification, or misuse of personal data. As a consequence, regulations such as the \acrshort{gdpr} impose strict requirements on how personal data must be processed and protected \cite{gdpr2016}. Organizations and individuals handling visual data must ensure that sensitive content is properly anonymized prior to publication, storage, or sharing.

Traditionally, anonymizing sensitive information in images has relied on manual editing using image processing software. While manual redaction provides a high degree of control, it is inherently time-consuming, repetitive, and prone to human error. These limitations become particularly significant when processing large volumes of visual material, where manual approaches become inefficient and difficult to scale \cite{piao2024deidentification}.

Recent advances in computer vision (\acrshort{cv}) have enabled automated techniques for detecting sensitive content in images. Many of these techniques rely on machine learning (\acrshort{ml}) models that can identify objects such as faces, license plates, and textual elements with increasing accuracy \cite{raina2023egoblur}. These developments create opportunities for more efficient \gls{anonymization} workflows that reduce manual effort while improving consistency. However, existing solutions still vary considerably in terms of detection accuracy, usability, and adaptability to different application contexts.

\section{Problem Statement and Scope}

Despite the availability of \acrshort{cv} techniques for detecting sensitive content, many existing solutions remain limited by challenges related to accuracy, usability, and privacy. Studies on privacy-preserving \acrshort{cv} systems highlight the difficulty of achieving reliable detection across diverse visual environments \cite{piao2024deidentification}. These limitations can be especially important when visual media contains personal or confidential information, as missed detections may leave sensitive content exposed. In addition, solutions that rely on remote processing may require sensitive images to be transferred to external infrastructure, which can introduce concerns related to trust, access control, and data exposure \cite{raina2023egoblur}. This makes \gls{privacy-by-design} principles important when designing systems for automated visual redaction.

Safe Media AI has developed an AI-based platform primarily designed for text-based document processing. As visual data continues to grow in importance, extending automated redaction capabilities to images represents a natural and necessary progression. However, designing an effective system for automated image redaction requires addressing challenges related to detection accuracy, usability, and privacy-by-design principles.

This project defines sensitive information in the context of visual media and uses this as the basis for designing \gls{SafeVision}. The system aims to automatically detect and anonymize sensitive information in visual media while maintaining efficiency, usability, and strong privacy protection. In addition to reliable detection and redaction, \gls{SafeVision} should provide flexible user interaction and interface design that allows users to review, adjust, and control the \gls{anonymization} process according to their specific workflow requirements.

Therefore the scope of the project is limited to the development and evaluation of a functional prototype for still image and video processing. The system will support common image and video formats and will be designed to integrate with the existing platform developed by Safe Media AI. The solution will also follow privacy-by-design principles and consider relevant data protection requirements, including \gls{gdpr}.

\section{Delimitation}

Based on the scope, several delimitations were necessary due to time, infrastructure, legal, and technical constraints. The following points clarify what is outside the scope of the project:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Audio processing is not supported.} 
    The system does not support redaction or \gls{anonymization} of audio. For video files, audio is removed entirely rather than processed separately. Standalone audio files for voice or sound redaction are therefore also outside the project scope.

    \medskip

    \item \textbf{The system is designed for \gls{cpu}-based execution.} 
    \gls{SafeVision} is developed and tested using Google Cloud services under a free-tier environment, which limits available computational resources. As a result, model \gls{inference} is designed to run without \gls{gdpr} support. This limitation is mainly caused by hardware availability and financial constraints.

    \medskip

    \item \textbf{Identity-specific recognition is not included.} 
    The project does not attempt to recognize specific individuals, vehicles, or other identity-specific entities. Such functionality would require additional development time and would introduce further \gls{gdpr}-related challenges. The system is therefore limited to detecting categories of sensitive content rather than identifying who or what a specific entity belongs to.

    \medskip

    \item \textbf{User authentication is outside the scope.} 
    Authentication is not implemented as part of the system. The solution is intended to integrate with the existing Safe Media AI platform, where authentication and access control are assumed to be handled separately.

    \medskip

    \item \textbf{Platform and device support is limited.} 
    The system is developed for the selected web-based deployment environment. Additional optimization for other device types, browsers, operating systems, or platforms is not included due to time constraints.

    \medskip

    \item \textbf{Model selection is limited by licensing.} 
    Only \acrshort{ml} models with licenses compatible with commercial use are considered. Models with restrictive licenses that prevent commercial deployment are excluded, even if they may be technically relevant.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Goals, Significance and Impact}
\label{sec:goals}

Appendix~\ref{app:project-plan} presents the original result, impact, and learning goals defined at the start of the project. During the project, these goals were refined to reflect the project outcomes described in Appendix~\ref{app:oppgave37} and the final system. The result goal was to design, develop, and evaluate a system for automatic and interactive redaction of sensitive content in visual media. In the final system, this includes detection and \gls{anonymization} of sensitive information in still images and video, support for user review and manual adjustment, and evaluation through both technical testing and user-oriented feedback.

The project also has academic and practical significance. By exploring \acrshort{cv} methods for automated \gls{anonymization}, the project contributes to work on privacy-preserving visual computing. Its combination of automated detection and user-guided refinement highlights the importance of balancing efficiency with human oversight, particularly in contexts where fully automated \gls{anonymization} may be unreliable or insufficient.

The impact goals were to reduce manual redaction work, lower the risk of human error, and improve privacy protection when visual material is prepared for publication or sharing. Beyond this, the project has practical value for Safe Media AI by supporting the extension of its platform from text-based document processing to visual media. In this way, the project provides a foundation for future development of more comprehensive privacy-preserving tools and demonstrates how such technology can be applied in real-world settings.

The learning goals were also central to the project. The work gave the group practical experience with artificial intelligence, \acrshort{ml} integration, computer vision, software development, agile methodology, collaboration, and system evaluation. These goals influenced both the technical implementation and the development process, including the use of Scrum-based routines, task distribution, model evaluation, user testing, and continuous documentation.

\section{Project Report Structure}

This thesis is organized to reflect the natural progression of the \gls{SafeVision} project, from problem definition and theoretical foundation to design, implementation, evaluation, and final reflection. The structure is intended to guide the reader through both the technical development of the system and the broader considerations related to privacy-preserving visual media processing.

\noindent\textbf{Foundation and Context (Chapters 1--3)}
This initial part establishes the foundation of the thesis. Chapter~\ref{chap:introduction} introduces the background, problem statement, project goals, delimitation, chosen approach, and significance of the work. Chapter~\ref{chap:theoretical_foundation} provides the theoretical and technical foundation by discussing sensitive information in visual media, \gls{gdpr}-related constraints, existing redaction workflows, \acrshort{ai}-based \gls{anonymization} methods, and the model choices relevant to \gls{SafeVision}. Chapter~\ref{chap:devprocess} then describes the development process, including the project methodology, sprint structure, stakeholder involvement, project timeline, and working practices.

\noindent\textbf{Requirements and Design (Chapters 4--6)}
These chapters present the requirements and design of the system. Chapter~\ref{chap:software_requirements_specification} defines the functional and non-functional requirements, use cases, and requirement prioritization. Chapter~\ref{system_architecture} presents the overall system architecture, including the main components, \acrshort{ml} pipeline, and deployment structure. Chapter~\ref{chap:user_interface_and_usability} presents the user interface (\acrshort{ui}) and usability considerations of \gls{SafeVision}. It first presents the theoretical foundations of user oriented interface design and then shows how these principles are applied in the final system to support user review, visual feedback, and manual control.

\noindent\textbf{Technology and Implementation (Chapters 7--8)}  
With the system design established, the thesis then presents the technologies and implementation. Chapter~\ref{chap:technologies_and_methodologies} describes the technologies and methodologies used in the project, including frontend, backend, \acrshort{ml}, storage, deployment, and infrastructure choices. Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation_details} provides the implementation details of the frontend, backend, \acrshort{ai}/\acrshort{ml} integration, redaction pipeline, video processing, and deployment setup.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation, Results, and Reflection (Chapters 9--13)}
The final part of the thesis evaluates the completed system and reflects on its outcomes. Chapter~\ref{chap:testing_system_evaluation} presents the testing and system evaluation, including frontend testing, backend testing, user testing, performance testing, and security evaluation. Chapter~\ref{chap:sustainability-analysis} discusses sustainability across human, social, environmental, economic, and technical dimensions. Chapter~\ref{chap:results} presents the results from the implemented models and processing pipeline. Chapter~\ref{chap:dar} discusses the project outcomes, including achieved goals, design decisions, limitations, ethical and privacy-related considerations, adoption perspectives, and future development opportunities. Finally, Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} concludes the thesis by summarizing the main contributions and findings.

The appendices provide supplementary material, including extended interface documentation, detailed model evaluation, training configurations, test coverage, metadata handling, the project plan, and selected meeting notes.
